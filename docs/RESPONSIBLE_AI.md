# Responsible AI and Inclusion

SautiForm Africa is intended to assist form completion, not to make legal or administrative decisions. The prototype therefore keeps the user in control of every structured record.

## Consent and privacy

Users should be told when audio is recorded, why it is needed and whether it will be retained. Benchmark audio must be consented and de-identified where possible. Raw recordings should not be committed to the public repository unless their licence and participant consent explicitly permit redistribution.

## Data minimisation

The first prototype collects only the fields needed for its demonstration workflow. It should not infer sensitive attributes or retain unrelated speech.

## Human confirmation

ASR and field extraction can fail, particularly under code-switching, accent variation and noise. SautiForm therefore does not treat model output as authoritative. Missing or ambiguous information triggers clarification, and a complete record is read back before any submission step.

## Fair benchmarking

Results should be reported by language pair and, where sample size permits, by accent/country, device and noise condition. Aggregate accuracy alone can hide systematic performance gaps.

## Limitations

A successful prototype does not establish suitability for production public services. Deployment would require domain-specific validation, accessibility testing, security controls, retention policies, redress mechanisms and consultation with the responsible public authority.
